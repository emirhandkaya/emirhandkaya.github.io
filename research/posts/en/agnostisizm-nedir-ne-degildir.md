## Overview

Etymologically, the term agnosticism derives from the Greek word "agnostos", meaning "unknown, unknowable".

Thomas Huxley said that he coined the word in a speech at the Metaphysical Society in England in 1869, in opposition to what Christians called "gnosticism", the claim of "having certain knowledge about the universe and God". However, although the term agnosticism was first used in 1869, it only appeared formally in a written work in 1878. Huxley regarded atheists and theists as being on the same level, and put forward the concept of agnosticism in opposition to all sects and beliefs.

In short, agnosticism, in its general sense, is a philosophically grounded view holding that human beings do not have the capacity and rational faculties required to prove the belief that God exists or does not exist, and that whether God exists cannot be known. According to an agnostic, the claims "God does not exist" and "God exists" are equal in standing.

But one should not think that agnosticism was invented by Thomas Huxley and has a history of only a few centuries. There were many agnostics in history before Huxley put forward this view. For example, the first agnostic was Protagoras. Although the word agnosticism had not yet been invented in his time, he showed an agnostic attitude in the philosophy found in his works:

> "Concerning the gods, I am unable to say either that they exist or that they do not exist; for there is much that prevents one from knowing this: the obscurity of the matter and the shortness of human life..."

This much etymological and historical information is enough to give a general idea of agnosticism; I will share more extensive, detailed and comprehensive writings with you later. Now let us look at the concepts classified as sub-branches of agnosticism.

## The Classification and Sub-Branches of Agnosticism

Some authors, for example William Rowe and Anthony Kenny, divided agnosticism into three: weak, moderate and strong. However, we can add the agnostic-theist and agnostic-atheist options as sub-divisions of weak agnosticism, and the concept of ignosticism as a somewhat higher division of agnosticism. Since the opportunity has come up, before the classification, let us clarify what terms such as ignostic, agnostic-theist and agnostic-atheist are. Ignosticism is etymologically derived from the English verb "ignore", whose meanings are "to disregard, to overlook, to not mind, to not care about". Ignostics hold a view such as "suspending a God whose possible existence we cannot know". However, this view should not be confused with apatheism. Fundamentally, for apatheists it makes no difference whether one believes in God or not; this is no different from wasting our time and killing time. Now let us come to the concepts of agnostic-theist and agnostic-atheist. To put it briefly without dragging on, agnostic-theism and agnostic-atheism mean that there is not enough evidence about God's existence, but that one is closer to existence or to non-existence. Agnostic-theists say that God's existence cannot be known, but that they are closer to the idea that He exists. Agnostic-atheists, on the other hand, express that God's existence cannot be known, but that they are closer to the idea that He does not exist.

Now it is the turn of the weak, moderate and strong agnostics that I mentioned at the beginning as the classification. Weak agnostics are people who are quite likely to change their mind about the knowability of God, and we can include views such as agnostic-theist and agnostic-atheist in this class as sub-divisions. Another view says: "there may not be enough evidence about God's existence right now, but if we invent very advanced technologies in the future, or manage to do it in some other way, we may find enough evidence about whether God exists or not." Moderate agnostics, who are generally the people we call agnostics and who hold this view, in other words middle or general agnostics, consider it possible that even though it is not known at present whether God exists or not, certain knowledge on this subject may be reached in the future. In short, they are not very determined that God cannot be known, and they are doubtful on this subject. Strong agnostics, however, dismiss such a view; that is, according to a strong agnostic, even if thousands or millions of years pass, or we invent extremely advanced technologies, or we attain an intelligence and perceptual capacity even higher than now, we still cannot reach certain knowledge about God's existence; in other words, whatever happens, we cannot know.

## What Agnosticism Is Not

Agnosticism is defined, mostly by dogmatic theists and also by some dogmatic atheists, as "being caught between two stools, hiding behind agnosticism instead of revealing whether one actually believes or not, or not knowing what to do". However, with the exception of the agnostic-theists and agnostic-atheists, about whom I will say a little, agnostics are not people caught between two stools or people who do not know what to do; agnostics are people who maintain that there is no sufficient and certain knowledge about whether God exists or not, and, in the case of strong agnostics, that this knowledge can never be reached. Although figures such as Richard Dawkins believe and argue that God is a subject of science and that His existence can be explained by science, this view is not quite correct. Because science deals with physical things whose existence has been proven; the idea of God, which is something non-physical, metaphysical (not perceptible by the senses, beyond the physical) and whose existence or non-existence has not been proven, cannot possibly be proven by science. Agnostics hold that, since something that cannot be proven by science is also something that human capacity or intelligence is unlikely to find evidence about, it would be wrong for me to say that people "can know" God's existence or non-existence merely from legends, myths or hearsay. So rather than "can know", we can only say "can believe".

In short, agnostics are not people who have not yet decided about their beliefs or religion; on the contrary, they are people who have decided, and who state their view on whether God exists as being agnostic, that is, unknowable.
