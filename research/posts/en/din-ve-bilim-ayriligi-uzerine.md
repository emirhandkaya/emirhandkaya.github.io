Can the claim made by some, that "there is no science without religion, and no religion without science", really hold? I will share my thoughts in detail shortly, but if I had to answer with a single word, it would certainly be this one: "nonsense".

### The Subjects Religion and Science Deal With

Before getting to the relationship between religion and science, it is better to examine the subjects and fields each one deals with.

By their own claims, religions say that God has communicated His wishes, laws and instructions, either in His own words or indirectly. In the simplest terms, religions promise definitive knowledge about existence, the universe and humankind; about birth and what precedes it, about life and what happens during it, about death and what follows it. In some religions this knowledge is passed down from generation to generation, while in others it is proclaimed to people through prophets or books. And these prophets perform miracles, that is, supernatural events, to prove that they are sacred. Religions tell people to believe in these sources and events, and say that whether they believe or not, there will be consequences such as reward or torment. The places where this reward or punishment takes place are called heaven and hell, and these places belong to what is called the belief in the afterlife. The afterlife is the resurrection of a person's soul, after death, in a different place or dimension.

Science, on the other hand, deals with physical events. It deals with topics such as matter, particles, space, atoms, the subatomic, organisms, cells and so on. Science involves experiment and observation, and is therefore open to testing. Knowledge that was once held to be true can be refuted by the results of experiments. That is, in science every piece of knowledge is true until it is refuted. Science is systematic, objective and consistent.

The U.S. National Academy of Sciences has stated: "Science is a way of knowing about the natural world. It is limited to explaining the natural world through natural causes. Science can say nothing about the supernatural. Whether God exists or not is a question about which science is neutral." Accordingly, it is clear that science has no satisfying answer to give in the face of miracles. In response to a miracle claim, science can only declare the impossibility of violating the laws of nature and offer a percentage-based account of how likely the miracles in question are to have actually occurred. By definition, a miracle lies beyond the laws of nature, logic and statistics; because, according to believers, the things we call miracles are performed directly by a being who determines the laws of nature and all the rules. Consequently, for God there is nothing "insurmountable". Some hold the view that "religious knowledge and events are not believed because they are true; they become true because they are believed."

As Maimonides put it:

> "Since all miracles become possible as soon as the world is accepted as having been created, the Law (Revelation) too becomes possible, and every problem we might raise on this matter is resolved."

Above, we briefly explained what they are and what they deal with. Now let us look at their differences, or their similarities, if there are any.

### The Main Differences Between Religion and Science

Religion contains metaphysical events, beings and knowledge, whereas science, by contrast, deals with physical phenomena and entities.

Religion cannot be tested, is true on its own terms and must be believed in, whereas science is open to testing, objective and experimental.

Religion and science deal with different subjects and are different things. They neither support nor refute each other. I hope these two major differences alone have explained quite well that religion and science are different things by the nature of their subjects.
